## **The 5 Biggest Wins**

**1\. Move your single best piece of proof to the top.**

A principal programmer at 37signals, the team that created Hotwire, recommends your book: *"Radan's Master Hotwire book... really recommend"* (Rosa Gutiérrez). That is the most credible endorsement a Hotwire book could possibly have, and it's sitting two-thirds of the way down in an undifferentiated wall, with no proof at all near the headline or the buy button.

* Put Rosa's quote directly under the headline and make the connection explicit: "recommended by a principal engineer at 37signals, the team that created Hotwire." Put a CTO quote (Miles Woodroffe, Mindful Chef) beside the buy button.  
* Risk-free, just do it.

**2\. The headline sells Hotwire; make it sell YOUR book.**

*"Build Modern Web Apps with Rails Simplicity"* is true of Hotwire itself, so it doesn't tell an experienced dev why to buy this book over reading the free docs. Your real edge is stated later and it's much sharper: *"as fluent with Hotwire as you are with Rails itself"* and *"debug issues in minutes, not hours."*

* Test a headline built on that mastery angle, for example: eyebrow *"For experienced Rails developers,"* headline *"Become as fluent in Hotwire as you are in Rails,"* subheadline about the under-the-hood understanding that lets you debug in minutes and build apps you can maintain for years.  
* Worth A/B testing against the current headline, because changing the main line can lift or dip conversions.

**3\. Put the guarantee at the buy button, not just in the FAQ.**

You offer a real 30-day money-back guarantee, but it only appears once, buried in the FAQ: *"you may request a refund any time within 30 days."* At €49 to a busy developer, that reassurance is exactly what tips a "maybe later" into a "yes now."

* Add a one-line "30-day money-back guarantee, no questions asked" under every *"Buy 'Master Hotwire' e-book for €49\!"* button. Keep the fuller version in the FAQ too.  
* Risk-free, just do it.

**4\. Answer the objection every dev is thinking: "why pay when the docs are free?"**

For a paid technical book, this is the biggest unspoken objection, and the page circles it (*"Official documentation and most online tutorials... explain how to use Hotwire... but it's not enough"*) without ever naming it head-on.

* Add it as an explicit beat, near the "deep understanding" section or as an FAQ: "Can't I just read the official docs?" Then your existing answer lands with full force: docs teach you how to use Hotwire, this teaches you how it actually works, so you pick the best approach and debug in minutes.  
* Risk-free, just do it.

**5\. Cut the repeated middle so the page moves faster.**

One idea recurs across roughly five sections: Hotwire is a *"sharp knife"* that can turn into *"spaghetti"* unless you understand it, so you can *"move fast... without giving up control"* with *"coding agents."* It shows up in "Can picking Hotwire be a mistake?", "I am trying to understand it," "What would it mean if fluent," "How would you implement this UI," and "Move fast without giving up control."

* Keep the sharpest instance of each idea and thin the rest. On a page written this well, the repetition is the main thing making it feel longer than it needs to be.  
* Risk-free, just do it.
